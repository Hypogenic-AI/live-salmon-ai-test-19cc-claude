**Title**  
**Decoding Predictors of Y: A Statistical Dissection of a 20-Feature Linear Dataset**

---

**Abstract**  
We analyze a dataset with 20 continuous features (X1–X20) and a continuous target Y (n=1000), using summary statistics, inter-feature and feature-target correlations, and OLS regression diagnostics. Our investigation reveals that Y is predominantly determined by X1, X3, X16, and X18, with strong evidence of collinearity and possible interaction effects among highly correlated feature pairs. The relationship between Y and its main predictors is primarily linear, but strong feature intercorrelations (notably X1–X16, X3–X18, X4–X19, and X5–X20) complicate the isolation of unique predictor effects. Model diagnostics suggest minor nonlinearity or heteroskedasticity for a subset of features. Our findings clarify which features are truly predictive of Y and highlight the statistical caveats inherent in such high-collinearity settings.

---

**Methods**  
We conducted a comprehensive statistical analysis using the provided summary statistics, feature-target and inter-feature correlations, and diagnostics from an OLS regression of Y on all features. The main analytic steps were:

1. **Screening for Predictiveness:**  
   - Examined feature–Y correlations to identify candidate predictors.
   - Assessed inter-feature correlations to detect multicollinearity.
2. **Regression Diagnostics:**  
   - Interpreted OLS model R² and the correlation between squared residuals and squared predictors to probe for possible nonlinearity or heteroskedasticity.
3. **Functional Form and Interactions:**  
   - Investigated the likelihood of linear vs. nonlinear relationships and potential interaction effects, especially among highly correlated feature pairs.
4. **Significance Assessment:**  
   - Used feature–Y correlations, adjusted for multiple comparisons (Bonferroni, α=0.05/20=0.0025), and considered multicollinearity in interpreting predictor significance.
5. **Effect Size Estimation:**  
   - Used correlation magnitudes as proxies for linear effect sizes due to lack of OLS coefficients.

---

**Results**

**1. Significant Predictors**

- Four features display strong, statistically significant correlations with Y (all p < 0.001, far exceeding Bonferroni threshold):

    | Feature | corr(Xi, Y) | Direction | Interpretation            |
    |---------|-------------|-----------|---------------------------|
    | X1      | +0.668      | Positive  | Strong main effect        |
    | X16     | +0.615      | Positive  | Strong main effect, but highly collinear with X1 (r=0.918) |
    | X3      | –0.538      | Negative  | Strong main effect        |
    | X18     | –0.413      | Negative  | Strong main effect, highly collinear with X3 (r=0.723)     |

- X2 (r=0.17) and X17 (r=0.12) show marginally significant but much weaker associations. All other features have negligible correlations with Y.

**2. Inter-feature Collinearity**

- Several predictor pairs have extremely high intercorrelations:
    - X1–X16: r=0.918
    - X3–X18: r=0.723
    - X4–X19: r=0.970
    - X5–X20: r=0.874
    - X2–X17: r=0.826

- This suggests that in any multiple regression, the unique effect of each member of a highly correlated pair cannot be disentangled; their joint effect can be estimated, but individual coefficients may be unstable.

**3. Functional Form & Interactions**

- The OLS model achieves a high R² = 0.789, indicating that nearly 80% of Y’s variance is explained by the 20 predictors.
- Correlations between squared residuals and squared predictors are generally low, except for X4 (0.36), X5 (0.37), X19 (0.35), and X20 (0.30). These four are not directly predictive of Y, but since X4 ≈ X19 and X5 ≈ X20, this pattern likely reflects model misfit (possible nonlinearity or heteroskedasticity) related to these collinear pairs.
- There is no strong evidence for meaningful nonlinear relationships between any Xi and Y; the main signal is linear.
- No direct evidence of interaction effects, but collinearity may mask possible interactions among X1/X16 and X3/X18.

**4. Effect Sizes (Estimated)**

- The standardized effect (correlation) of X1 and X16 with Y is large (r ≈ 0.6–0.7), as is that of X3 and X18 (r ≈ –0.5, –0.4).
- X2 and X17 have much smaller effects (r ≈ 0.1–0.17).
- All other features are effectively noise with respect to Y.

**5. Correction for Multiple Comparisons**

- After Bonferroni correction (α = 0.0025), only X1, X3, X16, and X18 remain significant.

---

**Discussion**

The data generating process for Y appears to be dominated by linear effects of X1 and X3, with X16 and X18 serving as proxies due to their high collinearity with X1 and X3, respectively. This is further supported by the extremely high inter-feature correlations within these pairs, meaning that Y could equivalently be predicted by either member of each pair. The high in-sample R² confirms that most of Y’s variability is captured by these few axes of variation.

The remaining features, including those with moderate residual-variance correlations (X4, X5, X19, X20), do not directly influence Y. Their association with model residuals likely reflects heteroskedasticity or a subtle, possibly nonlinear relationship not captured by the linear model, but not strong enough to make them true predictors.

No evidence was found for strong interaction effects; the functional form is primarily linear, dominated by two axes: (X1/X16) and (X3/X18), with a possible weak effect from (X2/X17).

**Which features matter?**
- X1/X16 (positive effect)
- X3/X18 (negative effect)
- Marginal: X2/X17 (weak positive effect)
- All others: effectively noise

---

**Limitations**

- The lack of OLS coefficients prevents precise quantification of each predictor's effect size and direction in a multivariate context, especially where multicollinearity is high.
- Collinearity between key features (e.g., X1/X16, X3/X18) means individual feature importance cannot be uniquely assigned—only their joint effect is reliably estimable.
- Nonlinear or interaction effects may be present but are not detectable from the supplied statistics.
- The analysis is based solely on summary statistics and diagnostics; more subtle effects or model mis-specification could go undetected.
- All findings pertain to in-sample behavior; out-of-sample predictive validity is untested.

---

**Conclusion**

In summary, Y is almost entirely explained by two underlying axes of variation—X1/X16 and X3/X18—with strong, opposing linear effects. Other features, including those with apparent residual associations, do not meaningfully predict Y. High feature collinearity is a major analytic challenge and precludes attribution of unique effects to individual members of correlated pairs. The model is robustly linear, with little evidence for nonlinearity or interaction among predictors. All conclusions are conditional on the data and statistics provided.
