# Title

**Disentangling the Structure of Predictive Relationships in a 20-Feature Regression Dataset: Evidence for Sparse, Linear, and Correlated Effects**

# Abstract

We analyzed a dataset with 20 continuous features (X1–X20) and a continuous target (Y, n=1000). Using correlation analysis, inter-feature diagnostics, and precomputed OLS regression model performance, we identified a sparse set of highly predictive features—primarily X1, X3, X16, and X18—and strong collinearity among selected pairs. The relationship between predictors and Y is predominantly linear, with minimal evidence for meaningful nonlinear or interaction effects. Our findings suggest that Y is generated by a linear combination of a few correlated predictors, with the majority of features contributing little explanatory power. We discuss the implications for model interpretability and highlight limitations stemming from collinearity and the absence of p-values in the supplied data.

# Methods

We conducted a rigorous statistical analysis using the following approaches:

1. **Exploratory Statistics**: Examined summary statistics and marginal (Pearson) correlations between each feature and Y.
2. **Collinearity Assessment**: Evaluated the correlation matrix and identified pairs with |r| > 0.7, signaling multicollinearity.
3. **OLS Model Diagnostics**: Interpreted supplied in-sample R² (0.7889) from an OLS regression with all 20 features, and inspected the correlation of squared residuals with squared predictors to assess potential heteroscedasticity or functional form misfit.
4. **Nonlinearity and Interactions**: Used the supplied 210 second-order (quadratic and cross-product) correlation coefficients with Y to detect evidence of nonlinear or interaction effects.
5. **Feature Selection**: Considered features as significant if their marginal correlation with Y was high (|r| > 0.1) and substantially exceeded those of the remaining features, and if their inclusion was supported by OLS model diagnostics.
6. **Multiple comparisons**: Noted the issue but did not apply formal correction, due to lack of p-values and explicit hypothesis testing in the supplied statistics.

# Results

## 1. Significant Predictors

- **X1**: r = 0.6675 with Y (very strong, positive).
- **X16**: r = 0.6145 with Y (very strong, positive), but X1 and X16 are highly collinear (r = 0.918).
- **X3**: r = -0.5379 with Y (strong, negative).
- **X18**: r = -0.4133 with Y (moderate, negative), highly collinear with X3 (r = 0.723).
- **X2**: r = 0.1722 with Y (weak, positive), highly collinear with X17 (r = 0.826).
- **X17**: r = 0.1187 with Y (weak, positive).

All other features have |r| < 0.1 with Y and are considered noise or non-predictive.

## 2. Collinearity and Redundancy

- **X1/X16** and **X3/X18** are highly redundant; including both in the model would introduce multicollinearity. Only one from each pair is necessary.
- **X2/X17** are also highly collinear, though their effect sizes are smaller.

## 3. Functional Form

- The high in-sample R² (0.7889) for the linear OLS model suggests that the relationship between Y and the significant predictors is overwhelmingly linear.
- The correlations of squared residuals with squared predictors are low for all but X4, X5, X19, and X20 (0.29–0.37), but these features themselves are not predictive of Y (|r| < 0.02), suggesting residual variance is not strongly related to the main predictors.
- Second-order term correlations (quadratic and interaction terms) with Y are uniformly small (all |r| < 0.27, with most < 0.1), indicating little evidence for meaningful nonlinear or interaction effects.

## 4. Estimated Effect Sizes

- Exact OLS coefficients are not supplied, but the sign and magnitude of the marginal correlations, along with the high collinearity within feature pairs, imply:
    - The effect of X1 (or X16) is strongly positive, with a standardized effect size similar to their correlation with Y.
    - The effect of X3 (or X18) is strongly negative.
    - X2 (or X17) may have a weak positive effect if included.
    - Other variables contribute negligibly.

## 5. Interaction and Nonlinear Effects

- The only second-order term with correlation |r| > 0.25 is X4*X5 (r = 0.2639) and X19*X20 (r = 0.2120), but X4/X5/X19/X20 themselves are not predictive of Y, suggesting this is due to chance or secondary structure rather than a true interaction relevant to Y.
- No evidence of meaningful nonlinear or interaction effects among the predictive features.

# Discussion

Our analysis reveals that the data generating process for Y is driven primarily by a sparse, nearly linear combination of X1 (or X16) and X3 (or X18), with possible minor contributions from X2 (or X17). All other features, including those with moderate to high collinearity (e.g., X4/X19, X5/X20), are not predictive of Y.

The extremely high R² of the linear OLS model (0.7889) indicates that the overwhelming majority of variance in Y is explained by a small set of features with strong linear relationships. The lack of significant nonlinearity or interactions, as evidenced by weak second-order correlations, further supports a simple, interpretable linear model as appropriate.

This structure—few dominant, highly collinear predictors and a majority of noise variables—is typical of data designed to test feature selection and regularization procedures, and emphasizes the importance of managing collinearity in regression modeling.

# Limitations

- **Collinearity**: High correlation among predictor pairs (e.g., X1/X16, X3/X18) makes it impossible to distinguish their individual effects; only their joint contribution to Y can be reliably estimated.
- **No p-values or CIs**: Supplied statistics do not include p-values or confidence intervals, so formal hypothesis testing and inference about statistical significance are not possible.
- **No out-of-sample validation**: All results are in-sample; no information is provided about model generalization or overfitting.
- **Interaction/Nonlinearity Detection**: Only Pearson correlations of second-order terms are reported, which may miss more complex functional forms.
- **Multiple Comparisons**: No formal correction applied; some weak correlations may be due to chance.
- **No model coefficients**: Without OLS coefficient estimates, effect sizes are inferred from correlations, which may be affected by collinearity and do not account for multivariate adjustment.

# Conclusion

The relationship between Y and the 20 features is overwhelmingly linear and sparse, dominated by X1/X16 (positive) and X3/X18 (negative), with possibly minor contributions from X2/X17. There is no evidence for substantial nonlinear or interaction effects, and most features are noise. These findings support the use of parsimonious, linear models with careful management of collinearity for this dataset.
